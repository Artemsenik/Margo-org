The path to an assignment

In seven steps

We do not accept every offer of help straight away – not out of reticence, but because poorly supervised help puts animals and people at risk.

  1. 1 · Short application

    A few sentences about you, your availability and what you can do.

  2. 2 · Review

    We check the need, the fit and whether we can support you properly.

  3. 3 · Introductory conversation

    Short, open, with room for your questions.

  4. 4 · First task

    Small and clearly defined – not everything at once.

  5. 5 · Briefing

    Safety, data protection and how to handle the animals.

  6. 6 · Review together

    What went well, what was difficult, what was missing?

  7. 7 · Decision

    Together: continue, adjust or stop.

What we do not promise: a guaranteed reference, guaranteed contact with animals or unlimited participation. What we do promise: an honest answer and a task that is genuinely needed.

What you take away

Helping here means building with us

Volunteering with us does not mean working through tasks nobody else wants. Those who help see at close range how private animal welfare turns into an organised system of relief – with roles, processes and traceable results. That is practical experience no course can give you: real responsibility in a small, serious project being built from the ground up.

A sense of impact

You see immediately that your contribution counts: animals fed, an appointment managed, a clear overview created.

Meaning instead of abstraction

Not „doing good somehow“, but concrete impact in a concrete place – week after week.

Practical skills

Route planning, care protocols, documentation, research, communication – depending on the role you learn craft that stays with you.

Project & process thinking

You experience how individual help becomes a system – and learn to make tasks repeatable and transferable.

Communication

Vets, partner organisations, supporters: you learn to work clearly and respectfully with very different people.

Real responsibility

Clearly bounded, but real: your role belongs to you – with everything that teaches you.

Belonging

You become part of a small team building something serious – with people who mean it as much as you do.

Encounters

Animals that learn to trust. Places you would otherwise never see. People who get things done. That stays with you.

Personal growth

Real situations, real decisions, visible results: you experience yourself as someone who does not just watch, but acts.

So that everyone is safe – animals, helpers, places

A few clear rules protect everyone involved. They are not an obstacle but the reason this works:

Animals are never taken away without approval.

Medical decisions are made by vets together with Margo – never by volunteers.

Sensitive location data is not shared publicly.

Photos, videos and stories are published only after approval.

New helpers start with one clear, small task.

Reliability matters more than perfect experience.
