A clear division of roles

  • Margo on site: daily care, decisions about each individual animal, approval of every rehoming.
  • The initiative: documentation, cost records, coordination of volunteers and communication.

What funds are needed for

Four cost areas carry the ongoing work. A complete breakdown is now available for veterinary care; the remaining areas are currently being recorded.

Care takes place in the Artemida/Athens region: a farm, several private care locations and outdoor feeding colonies. Exact locations are not published, to protect the animals and private households.

Food

The largest ongoing item: dry and wet food for all care locations and colonies – every day, in any weather. Four pet food shops already grant discounts; the quantities remain considerable nonetheless.

Veterinary treatment

Examinations, medication, operations and emergencies. Two partner practices treat at reduced rates.

Documented: 32 invoices totalling €3,954.70 between 23 March and 20 July 2026 – around €1,000 per month.

Core task

Neutering: the lever that reduces costs

Feeding keeps animals alive. Neutering stops new ones from constantly arriving. That is why neutering is not a side task for us, but the most effective use of every euro donated.

Why this makes every contribution more sustainable

An unneutered pair of cats can produce a double-digit number of offspring within a few years. Each of those animals then needs food, water and medical care every day – permanently.

Every neutering reduces future food requirements and future veterinary costs. Funding a neutering is therefore not a recurring expense but a one-off investment that permanently lowers the need.

The concrete need: neutering the colonies completely

Every cat living permanently with Margo – on the farm, in the family home, at the second residence or at the surf school – is neutered. In the free-living colonies this has so far happened only where an animal needed medical treatment anyway. By Margo's estimate, around 150 colony cats are still unneutered.

The reason is not a lack of willingness but a lack of money. At the stated flat rate of €50, this would amount to a funding need of roughly 7.500 € – a rough order of magnitude, not a fixed budget, since Margo has not yet set a timeframe or a specific campaign.

The path of a donation

How your donation reaches Margo

Donations run through the account of Murka-Katzenhilfe e. V. The association does not use sub-accounts but works with earmarked payments – allocation happens via the payment reference.

1. State the payment reference

So that your donation is allocated to Margo's work, use one of these keywords as the payment reference:

  • Griechenland
  • Athen
  • or the name of a specific cat

2. Allocation within the association

Murka-Katzenhilfe e. V. allocates the payment to the work in Greece based on the reference. Without a reference the donation goes into the association's general work.

3. Tax-deductible

Donations through the association's account can be claimed for tax purposes. Direct payments to Margo personally are not deductible.

If tax deductibility matters to you, please make the payment through the account of Murka-Katzenhilfe e. V.
